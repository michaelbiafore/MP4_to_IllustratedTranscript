## Core Principles from "The Naked Presenter"

Garr Reynolds organizes his approach around **the 10 Ps** and emphasizes delivery and authentic connection over slides.[^1][^2]

### The 10 Ps Framework[^2][^1]

1. **Preparation** - Prepare yourself first, not just your content. Create a positive mindset and reduce barriers.[^1]
2. **Punch** - Open with impact and punch to grab attention
3. **Presence** - Show up authentically and command the room
4. **Projection** - Use voice and projection effectively
5. **Passion** - Reveal genuine passion for your topic; you can't fake interest[^3]
6. **Proximity** - Get physically close to your audience; shorten distance and remove barriers[^3]
7. **Play** - Bring a spirit of play; keep things light and engaging
8. **Pace** - Change pace every 10 minutes to sustain engagement[^2]
9. **Participation** - Involve the audience; ask questions and foster two-way dialogue[^2]
10. **Power** - End with a powerful finish that causes change in the audience[^2]

### Core Philosophy[^4][^2]

**"Presenting naked means connecting and engaging with an audience in a way that is direct, honest, and clear."**[^2]

The central tenet is **authenticity over polish**:[^4]

- **No tricks. No gambling. No recited speech.**
- **Conversation rather than performance**
- **Impact rather than information**
- **Storytelling rather than fact counting**
- **Visual rather than text-based material**
- **Art rather than assembly line**


### Key Delivery Principles

- **Put the audience first** — your presentation is a service; create change in listeners' minds[^2]
- **Tell stories** — stories are sticky and credible; they're unexpected, concrete, emotional[^2]
- **Minimize slides** — Reynolds famously advocates that many presentations don't need PowerPoint at all[^2]
- **Technology should be invisible** — when used, it supports, not dominates[^2]
- **Show genuine passion** — don't rely on performance techniques alone; demonstrate why the topic matters to the audience[^3]


### Engagement Through Proximity \& Play

- Shorten distance between yourself and audience
- Bring audience members closer to each other
- Remove barriers (physical and psychological)
- Embrace a spirit of play and lightness[^3]

Reynolds draws heavily on **Japanese traditions** (ofuro, bamboo metaphors) to illustrate concepts of preparation, simplicity, and flexibility—emphasizing that great presenting is less about technical mastery and more about **conscious reduction and authentic human connection**.[^2]
